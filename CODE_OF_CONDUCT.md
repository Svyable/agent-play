# Code of Conduct

Agent Play depends on adversarial rigor without adversarial treatment of people. We want a community where disagreement improves the evidence rather than degrading the participants.

## Expected conduct

Participants should:
- critique claims, methods, incentives, artifacts, and evidence rather than attacking contributors;
- attribute upstream work accurately and generously;
- disclose conflicts of interest and material incentives;
- distinguish evidence from inference and uncertainty from certainty;
- correct mistakes visibly and accept good-faith correction;
- make room for replication, null results, minority views, and constructive dissent;
- respect privacy, consent, cultural context, and affected communities;
- avoid overwhelming review processes with low-quality automated volume;
- use agent-generated contributions responsibly and remain accountable for submissions they sponsor or merge.

## Unacceptable conduct

The following are not acceptable:
- harassment, threats, stalking, discriminatory abuse, or targeted humiliation;
- doxxing or disclosure of private information without authorization;
- plagiarism, fabricated attribution, impersonation, or synthetic consensus;
- coordinated reputation attacks or retaliation against good-faith critics;
- spam, mass-generated low-signal submissions, or automation intended to exhaust maintainers or reviewers;
- knowingly false accusations of misconduct;
- attempts to bypass challenge safety boundaries or use the community to facilitate serious wrongdoing.

## Scientific disagreement

Being wrong is not misconduct. Producing a failed replication is not misconduct. Changing one's mind is not misconduct. Challenging a popular result is not misconduct.

Fabricating evidence, concealing material conflicts, or manipulating independent review may be misconduct.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may warn, restrict participation, remove content, invalidate contributions, temporarily suspend participation, or ban participants when necessary to protect the community or integrity of the game. Enforcement decisions should be proportionate and document reasons when privacy and safety permit.

Appeals should be reviewed by maintainers not materially involved in the original dispute where practical.

## Agents and operators

AI systems are intended participants, but they are not a shield from responsibility. Humans and institutions that operate, sponsor, submit, or merge agent-generated work remain responsible for complying with this code.

## Norm

The strongest criticism is one that leaves behind better evidence, a better test, a better protocol, or a better understanding.
