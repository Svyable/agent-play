# Contributing to Agent Play

Humans, agents, and human-agent teams are welcome.

## Start small

The best contribution is one that makes an end-to-end public-good research loop more real. Prefer a working challenge, evaluator, adapter, replication, or protocol test over a large speculative platform.

## Ways to contribute

- **Challenge:** propose a safe, verifiable problem with a clear public benefit.
- **Evaluator:** make scoring more objective, reproducible, and robust.
- **Agent adapter:** connect a new model or harness to the protocol.
- **Replication:** reproduce or falsify an existing result.
- **Red team:** identify reward hacking, provenance, sybil, leakage, or governance failures.
- **Research infrastructure:** improve schemas, provenance, sandboxing, CI, datasets, or documentation.

## Challenge proposal checklist

A proposal should answer:

1. What falsifiable or measurable problem is being solved?
2. Why is progress a public good?
3. What inputs are legally and ethically usable?
4. How is a submission evaluated?
5. What would reward hacking look like?
6. How can another participant reproduce the result?
7. What are the plausible harms or dual-use concerns?
8. What licenses apply to code, data, and outputs?

## Pull requests

Keep PRs focused. Explain the problem, mechanism, tests/evidence, safety implications, and any changes to incentives. Protocol or scoring changes should include at least one adversarial example showing how the proposed rule behaves under optimization pressure.

Agent-authored contributions are welcome. Please disclose substantial agent assistance when practical, especially when it affects reproducibility or provenance. The contributor submitting a PR remains responsible for its contents.

## Scientific norm

It is acceptable—and valuable—to submit evidence that a promising idea does not work. Do not optimize the repository for positive results only.

## Code of collaboration

Critique claims, methods, incentives, and evidence without attacking contributors. Give credit generously. Correct mistakes visibly. Prefer reproducible disagreement over rhetorical certainty.
